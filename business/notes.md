# Business notes (private)

**This is not legal or tax advice.** Rules and prices change. Check gov.uk, HMRC and ICO, and speak to an accountant or solicitor before relying on anything here.

## Do these before your first paying client
- [ ] **Separate business email** for client messages and client-related accounts, kept apart from your personal email and any other business you run.
- [ ] **Cloudflare and GitHub:** own passwords, 2FA, recovery codes stored safely (`docs/04`).
- [ ] **HMRC:** check whether you need to register or update your Self Assessment for this new income. Search gov.uk for "check if you need to tell HMRC about your income", and ask about the trading allowance and how it interacts with your existing self-employment.
- [ ] **Separate records** for this income and costs, ideally a separate bank account.
- [ ] **Insurance:** ask a broker about **professional indemnity** and public liability for web design. A policy for another business is unlikely to cover it.
- [ ] **Data protection:** check at ico.org.uk whether you need to pay the data protection fee, since you'll hold client details.
- [ ] **Trading name:** check it's not already in use, and follow the rules for business names on invoices.

## Pricing ideas (test them; these aren't market data)
Before setting prices, look at what local freelancers and agencies charge.

| Model | How it works | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **One-off** | One fee, changes charged after | Clients who rarely change anything | No repeat income, and hosting you still look after |
| **Build + care plan** | Lower or normal build, plus a monthly fee | Most small businesses | Be clear what's included |
| **Tiers** | Starter, standard, plus | Making the choice easy | Keep the differences simple |

Example tiers to adapt:
- **Starter:** home page, up to 4 sections' worth of services, 10 photos.
- **Standard:** full home page, FAQ, 20 photos, reviews.
- **Plus:** extra pages, video, price tables.

**Track your real hours** on the first few jobs, then adjust. Charge for your time, including messages and revisions.

## What's included (decide and keep it consistent)
- Number of pages and photos
- **Revision rounds:** 2 is a sensible start. Define a round as "one list of changes sent together".
- What counts as an extra, and your hourly rate
- Care plan contents, such as a monthly allowance of small changes

## Contract checklist
- [ ] Scope, and what's **not** included
- [ ] Price, deposit, payment dates, late payment
- [ ] Timeline, and what happens if the client goes quiet
- [ ] Revision limit
- [ ] Client confirms they own or have rights to their content, and that it's accurate
- [ ] Who owns what, and when (usually after final payment)
- [ ] Domain in client's name
- [ ] Hosting terms, exit plan, "if I'm unavailable"
- [ ] No guarantee of rankings, leads or sales
- [ ] Liability limit (take advice on the wording)
- [ ] Cancellation by either side
- [ ] Data protection

## Data rules of thumb
- Hold only what you need, and delete it when it's no longer needed.
- **Never collect client passwords.** Ask them to add you as a user, or sit with them.
- Keep client files off shared devices. Use 2FA on anything holding client data.
- Reviews and photos of people need consent. Keep a copy of that consent.

## Protecting yourself
- Get the deposit **before** starting.
- Keep every approval in writing.
- Don't publish until the balance is paid (or agree otherwise).
- Never put a domain in your own name, even to "help out".
- Keep client sites from depending on your personal accounts wherever you can.

## Finding clients
- Show a real site you've already built as the showcase.
- Ask happy clients for a referral and a review.
- Local businesses with no site, or a poor one on mobile, are the easiest conversation. Follow the rules on direct marketing, and be honest and polite.
